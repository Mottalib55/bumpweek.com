How Much Does Baby Formula Cost?

Baby formula costs between $55 and $250 per month depending on the brand you choose. Store brands meet the exact same FDA nutritional standards as name brands — at 30-50% lower cost. Here's how the numbers break down.

Ready-to-feed vs powder

Formula comes in three forms, and the price differences are dramatic:

  • Powder: Cheapest option at $0.56-$1.79/oz depending on brand. Requires mixing with water. Annual cost: $700-$2,800.
  • Concentrated liquid: Middle ground. Mix with equal parts water. About 1.5x the cost of powder. Annual cost: $1,200-$4,000.
  • Ready-to-feed: Most convenient — no mixing needed. Perfect for travel and nighttime feeds. But 2-3x the cost of powder. Annual cost: $2,500-$6,000.

Most families use powder at home and keep ready-to-feed bottles in the diaper bag for outings.

How to save on formula

  • Switch to store brands: Kirkland (Costco), Parent's Choice (Walmart), and Up & Up (Target) all meet the same FDA nutritional standards as Enfamil and Similac. Annual savings: $700-$1,000.
  • Buy in bulk: Costco and Sam's Club offer the lowest per-ounce prices. A Costco membership ($65/year) pays for itself in 2 months of formula savings.
  • Sign up for manufacturer rewards: Enfamil Family Beginnings and Similac StrongMoms send free samples and coupons. Sign up for both during pregnancy.
  • Check WIC eligibility: The WIC program covers formula for families up to 185% of the federal poverty level (about $57,720 for a family of 4). WIC covers about half of all formula sold in the US.
  • Amazon Subscribe & Save: 5-15% off with automatic delivery.
  • Never buy from unofficial resellers. Formula from unknown third parties may be expired, counterfeit, or improperly stored.

The breastfeeding vs formula financial comparison

The honest math on both options:

Breastfeeding costs

  • Breast pump: $0 (often covered by insurance in the US) to $300
  • Storage bags: $80-$150/year
  • Nursing bras: $50-$100
  • Nursing pads: $30-$60/year
  • Total: $0-$600/year

Formula costs

  • Store brand powder: $700-$1,000/year
  • Name brand powder: $1,200-$2,800/year
  • Organic/premium: $1,800-$3,500/year

Breastfeeding is cheaper financially. But time has value too — the hours spent pumping, cleaning parts, and managing supply are real costs that don't show up on a spreadsheet. There's no wrong choice here. Feed your baby in the way that works for your family.
